The proposition

One register, not three reports.

A stolen laptop is a physical failure and a data breach. A dismissed employee who keeps a badge is a personnel matter, an access control matter and an audit finding. Split the assessment across three vendors and each writes up their own third.

Enterprise Security Risk Management treats them as one portfolio: assets ranked by the business, threats mapped across every vector at once, and the residual risk accepted by a named owner rather than left ambiguous between departments.

The ESRM cycle

Security decisions belong to the asset owner; the practitioner’s job is to make the risk legible and the choice explicit. Four steps, repeating.

STEP 01

Identify & prioritise assets

What the organisation actually values — people, premises, data, continuity, reputation — ranked by the business, not by the security team.

STEP 02

Identify & prioritise risks

Threats mapped to those assets across every vector at once: intrusion, insider, outage, breach, disruption.

STEP 03

Mitigate prioritised risks

Controls chosen against risk and budget, with the residual risk stated plainly and accepted by name.

STEP 04

Continuous improvement

Incidents, audits and near-misses fed back into the register. Anything unresolved is carried forward, not dropped.
